# PJBoy — The Lore Bible

*The canonical story for **Asteroid mode** (the PJBoy universe). This is the cozy canon: it keeps the whole world, the full 12-character cast, the six worlds, the Wardens, and the four-part mystery — but every beat lands **gentle, hopeful, and age-appropriate**, in line with the audience north-star in `CLAUDE.md` (a fun, gently challenging, educational game built for a 7-year-old, biased toward science and animals). Where this and any older draft disagree, **this file wins.***

> One guiding rule for all writers: nothing here should ever frighten or sadden a small child. The worlds **fall asleep**, they do not die. Friends **drift off**, they are not lost. The builders **became the stars**, they did not run out. The whole game is about turning the lights back on.

---

## The one line

You are a young **Kindler**. The worlds are getting sleepy and quiet. Relight them, wake everyone up, bring them home — and find out why a kid is the one who can hear the song.

---

## 1. The Hum and the Hush

Long ago the **Ancients** built six worlds and hung them in a ring around a single bright heart. They were not gods — they were **builders**, the best there ever were. When their work was done, they left the worlds to grow on their own.

What keeps every world alive is the **Hum**: a low, warm song running through the stone, the water, and the roots. You cannot see it. Everything alive can *feel* it — it is the sound of a world that is awake and happy.

Lately the Hum has been getting quiet. Where the song fades, the **Hush** drifts in: a soft, sleepy, forgetful quiet, like a whole world nodding off with the lights low. The Hush is not a monster and it did not invade. It is simply what happens when a world's song winds down and no one has wound it back up. Creatures caught in the Hush go drowsy and dim and a little grumpy, the way anyone does when they are sleepy and lonely in the dark. We call them the **Hushed** — and every one of them is just someone waiting to be woken.

In each world the Ancients left a giant guardian, a **Warden**, to watch over it. With the Hum so faint, the Wardens have drifted too. Some have fallen asleep. Some guard too hard, because guarding is the last thing they remember how to do. Some have gone dim and need waking like everyone else.

You are a **Kindler**: a kid who can still hear the Hum when almost no one else can. That is your whole power — and it turns out to be the only one that matters.

---

## 2. The factions

- **The Living** (green). The native creatures and the biome peoples. Most are shy or kind; a few are huge and gentle. These are the families you bring home.
- **The Ancients** (cyan/gold). The builders and their machines. **Lumen** keeps the Waystation lit. **Sentinels** still patrol on old, tangled orders. Warden-constructs guard the worlds and test you.
- **The Hushed** (soft violet). Anyone — creature, machine, or villager — who has drifted into the sleepy Hush. They are **not evil; they are just dim and dreaming**. Relighting a world wakes them, and a warm light close up can gently bring them back to themselves.

---

## 3. Totems — the heart of the loop

Each people has a **Totem**: a small voxel relic that *is* them. You find or craft it, carry it home, and **plant it in the Village**. A planted totem does four cozy things at once: it lights that people's district, calls their friend home, grants a passive boon, and writes a line in the Codex. Totems are how **collecting, building, befriending, and learning** all become one happy action.

Six peoples, six worlds, six totems:

| Totem | People | World | Looks like | Boon when planted | Its line |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Greenheart** | the Mossfolk | Verdant | a knot of living wood with a slow pulse | saplings regrow; Verdant blooms faster | *"It was a seed when the Hum was loud. It is waiting to be loud again."* |
| **Tideshell** | the Driftfolk | Aquaria | a spiral shell holding a caught tide-light | longer breath underwater; current-sight | *"Hold it to your ear. That is the sound of the sea, humming in its sleep."* |
| **Gilllight** | the Sporefolk | Mycelia | a glowing gill-cap on a pale stem | light in the dark; reveals spore-paths | *"One light, shared a thousand ways. That is how the Hollow remembers itself."* |
| **Threetail** | the Frostkin | Frostpeak | a carved frost-fox, breathing mist | warmth aura; resists Hush-chill | *"Where the three tails point, the snow is thin. Where they still, turn back and warm up."* |
| **Wayfinder** | the Dustnomads | Dustfall | a bone compass that leans toward buried things | reveals buried Ancient ruins; sandstorm sight | *"The Dust hides the old roads. The Wayfinder remembers them."* |
| **Emberkin** | the Cinderpack | Ember | a small hound-fire brazier that never scalds | resists heat and lava; wakes the cinder-pup | *"A fire that does not burn what it loves. The warmest thing in all of Ember."* |

**The Ancient relics** (a tier above the totems; not planted in a district):

- **The Lantern.** Lumen's, and the Village's. The first light — the one you rekindle at the Hearth. It is the reason the Waystation never went fully dark.
- **The six Worldcores.** The great relics, one per world, each kept by a Warden. They go into the Aetherheart at the very end, not in the Village.
- **Pip's Spark.** A caught fleck of Aether in a jar. Nobody knows quite what it is — not even Pip. (See the Mysteries.)

**The anti-totem.** The sleepy Hush leaves little markers too: **Hush-shards**, dim and cold, that root into a world and spread the quiet outward. Clearing a Hush-shard is the exact opposite of planting one of your totems — the game's two mirror-image verbs. A deeply-Hushed world is one where the dim shards still outnumber your lights.

## 4. The Village — your home, and the point

Between worlds you come home to the **Waystation**: a sleepy old Ancient outpost floating near the heart of everything. It is not just a menu hub — it is a **settlement that fills up with the friends you wake.** It starts as a dim ring of ruins around a banked fire. You rebuild it block by block, totem by totem, friend by friend.

**The Hearth** (center). The great **Lantern** sits here, glowing low. Rekindling it is your very first act and the Village's heartbeat. Lumen tends it. This is the (local co-op) spawn and the safe, warm light at the middle of the world.

**The six districts**, each dim until you bring its totem home — then it lights up, claims its quarter, and its people move in:

- **The Grove** — Mossfolk. Bramblewick and the Mosshorns. Lit by **Greenheart**.
- **The Drift** — Driftfolk. Quill's **Workshop** sits here (gear, repairs, totem-crafting). Lit by **Tideshell**.
- **The Hollow** — Sporefolk. Lit soft and blue by the **Gilllight**.
- **The Frosthold** — Frostkin. Floe curls up by the warm-stone. Lit by **Threetail**.
- **The Wayrest** — Dustnomads. A traders' tent of buried finds. Lit by **Wayfinder**.
- **The Forge & Kennel** — Cinderpack. Tinder and the pups. The **Emberkin** brazier.

**Shared buildings**, rebuilt from what you mine:

- **The Codex Hall.** Every block, creature, and Echo you ever scanned, archived. Finishing it is its own warm reward.
- **The Echo Well.** Replay any Ancient memory you found. Where the story lives once you have heard it.
- **The Gate.** Travel to any world you have relit. Worlds you have woken are calm and bright; worlds you have not are still drowsy.
- **The Ringwall.** The Village's friendly defense. Leave it half-built and, while you are away, drowsy Hushed will wander in looking for the light — a gentle, low-stakes tower-defense beat (no one gets hurt; you shoo them awake). Plant totems, raise the wall, and they stop wandering in. (Local co-op shines here.)

**Why it matters.** The Village is the cozy reward for every trip out: leave a sleepy world, come home, and watch your home and your found family light up one window at a time. For a kid, *that loop is the whole game.*

## 5. The cast, in canon

### Allies — The Kindled

- **Lumen** *(formerly "the Curator")* — the last Ancient construct still awake, a lantern-keeper who tends the Hearth. He is your mentor and the Village's memory. He speaks slowly, in long, kind pauses. **He guides you out in the worlds as a flickering projection, and waits for you back home as the lantern-keeper** — the same being, two forms. He kept the light burning but cannot quite remember building the worlds, and that little gap is a thread you can gently pull.
- **Pip** — your companion: another Kindler kid, a half-step braver than is wise. Carries the **Spark** in a jar and a hundred questions. Your first friend, and the one who will never leave. Came from Verdant.
- **Bramblewick** — the **Verdant guide**. A shy Mossfolk sprite who knows every hidden grove-path. Leave a kindness, and find a road cleared in the morning.
- **Floe** — the **Frostpeak guide**. A three-tailed frost-fox spirit; the only thing that moves surely through a whiteout. Loves a loud, happy world and flinches at too much quiet.
- **Quill** — the **Aquaria tinker**, and the Village's smith once you befriend it. A hermit crab in a borrowed shell and a worse mood, with the finest hands in the tide-pools. Mention the Tidemother and the grumbling stops.
- **Tinder** — an **Ember** cinder-pup that bounded up to you and decided you were its person before the Hush could make it drowsy like the rest of its litter (who are only sleeping, and whom you can wake). Warm, fierce in your defense, leaves little fading flame-prints.
- **Myco** *(proposed, not yet modeled)* — the **Mycelia** ally: a small Sporefolk lantern-bearer who keeps the Hollow lit.
- **Sift** *(proposed, not yet modeled)* — the **Dustfall** ally: a Dustnomad wayfinder who reads the sand.

### The Hushed — sleepy, not scary (and one tangled-up Ancient)

These are your obstacles, but the design rule is firm: **they are drowsy and dim, never cruel.** You wake them, soothe them, and shoo them along; you never harm them. A warm light close up can bring a Hushed friend all the way back.

- **Hushling** — the little one. Dim-chested, quiet, twitchy with sleep. Never wanders alone. You hear the patter of the others first; the Hushling itself is silent, and that is the tell.
- **Sleeper** *(the cast file calls this model "Husk")* — **the warmest thread in the game.** Before you arrived, the Waystation had people: the first Kindlers and the Ancients' helpers. The Hush drifted over them and they nodded off, wandering away half-dreaming. The stooped, reaching shapes you meet out in the worlds are **them** — looking for the song they fell asleep to, and hoping you might be it. Relight a world and plant its totem, and a few Sleepers **remember their own name, wake up, and walk home.** Repopulating the Village is literally waking the Hush, one friend at a time.
- **Mute** — a drifting silence-moth whose wingbeats turn the volume of a world down low and spread the sleepy quiet further. It drifts toward the bright Spark in Pip's jar. (Annoying, not dangerous.)
- **Gnash** — a big creature that fell deep asleep mid-prowl and now stalks its own dream. Faster and more focused than the little Hushed. It needs a brighter, braver wake-up than the rest.
- **Riftling** — not drowsy but **frazzled**: a flicker of jumbled pieces that pops out wherever the quiet has pulled a world's seams loose. It means the Hush has gotten deep enough to start unstitching a world, and it is the gentle "ticking clock" that tells you a world needs you soon. (See the Mysteries.)
- **Sentinel** — an Ancient watcher-drone, still running long after its makers rose into the sky. It is **not Hushed** — its old orders have just gotten tangled, and now it reads every living thing as something to log and tidy away. It is doing exactly what it was built to do, and no one is left to tell it "all done, you can rest now." Untangling its orders settles it.

### The Wardens — your six guardians

Wordless. The feeling does the talking. Two are **Living** and a little overprotective (not unkind — they just guard too hard). Four have gone **dim** and need a good waking.

| World | Warden | Faction | The encounter |
|---|---|---|---|
| Verdant | **Grovekeeper** | Living | Tests you kindly, then hands over the Worldcore freely. A gentle first "boss." |
| Aquaria | **Tidemother** | Hushed | Drifts close with her heart-light, half-dreaming. Light up her bell to wake her and she calms. |
| Mycelia | **Sporewarden** | Living (wild) | A forest pretending to be a body. Earn its attention without getting tangled in blooms. |
| Frostpeak | **Rimewyrm** | Hushed | The first real test of skill. Coax its frost-breath into the Hush-ice to crack the dim crust and wake it. |
| Dustfall | **Gravemaw** *(to build)* | Hushed | A sand leviathan, deep asleep under the dunes. Read the sand-tells, dodge the breach, and light it up when it surfaces. |
| Ember | **Pyroclast** | Hushed | The grumpiest and the last. Cool its overheated heart by plugging the vents, then bring the light to wake it. |

## 6. The Mysteries

The story is never told in walls of text. It comes out through **Echoes** (scannable little memory-motes, 6–8 per world), through what the Wardens make you *feel*, and through Lumen's short, kind, half-finished lines. Four mysteries run the spine; the rest are gentle threads to pull on replays.

### The spine (intended reveal order)

1. **Why is the Hum getting quiet?** *(Act 1, Verdant.)* The surface question. The first Echoes only show that the Ancients stopped feeding the song — not why.
2. **What is the Hush, really?** *(Act 2, the middle worlds.)* It is not an enemy that arrived. It is the soft, sleepy quiet that settles wherever a builder's light burned low. Every Hushed thing is a friend who dozed off there — and every one can be woken.
3. **Where did the Ancients go?** *(Late Act 2 → Act 3.)* The wistful turn: they poured their own light into the worlds to keep them singing, and when they had given nearly all of it, they rose together into the sky and **became the stars.** They are still up there, watching. They left the worlds — and the work — for whoever could still hear the song.
4. **What are you?** *(The finale.)* You are a Kindler, but the idea is older than the word. The Ancients built, on purpose, a way for a child who could hear the Hum to **carry on the work after them.** The **Architect** at the Aetherheart is not an enemy and not a ghost — it is a warm, waiting **empty chair**. You were never sent to rescue the builders. You were invited to *become* one.

> The landing is always hopeful: the old builders left you a whole universe and a note that says *your turn.*

### The threads (optional, replayable, expansion-ready)

| Thread | The hook | Where it pays off |
|---|---|---|
| **The first Kindlers** | You are not the first child they invited. Some of the deepest Sleepers were Kindlers before you. Where did they wander off to? | Hidden Echoes; waking certain Sleepers by name. |
| **The Sentinel's orders** | Each Sentinel carries a tangled fragment of the Architect's last, kind instruction. Collect the fragments. | Piece the Architect's last words back together before you meet it. |
| **The Riftling clock** | The quiet used to just make things sleepy. Now it tugs seams loose. The worlds need waking sooner rather than later. | Sets up post-launch / Act 4 adventures. |
| **Pip's Spark** | What is in the jar? A seed of a brand-new Worldcore? A piece of the Hum that chose a kid? Pip honestly does not know. | The Aetherheart finale — the Spark may be exactly what relights it. |
| **Lumen's gap** | The keeper kept the light but forgot building the worlds. Why? | He gave those memories to the Hum too — a keeper who spent a little of himself to stay awake for you. |
| **The gentle Wardens** | Grovekeeper and Sporewarden never went dim. They still remember the Ancients fondly. | Deep, optional lore told (wordlessly) by the Living Wardens. |

## 7. The mission structure (how it all interlocks)

Every world runs the same three cozy phases. The systems above slot straight in.

- **Phase 1 — ARRIVE.** Land, settle in, learn the world. Gather its two signature blocks, scan three natives, place a beacon-stone. *(Meet the world's guide: Bramblewick, Floe, Quill, Myco, Sift, or Tinder.)*
- **Phase 2 — RESTORE.** Push the sleepy quiet back. Build the world's Beacon, clear Hush-shards, and find and scan the Echoes. *(This is where you recover that world's **Totem**.)*
- **Phase 3 — WARDEN.** Follow the waking Hum to the guardian. Wake / soothe / win it over. Take the **Worldcore**, head home. *(Plant the totem; a district lights up; Sleepers walk home.)*

**Mission verbs:** GATHER, BUILD, SCAN, CLEANSE (mine the dim Hush-shards), PROTECT (keep a beacon or a creature lit for a timer), PUZZLE (follow the shared underground geology and ore veins; open Ancient doors), WARDEN (the guardian beat), and DEFEND (a gentle Ringwall wake-up at an under-built Village).

**The finale — the Aetherheart.** With all six Worldcores in hand, you descend. Socket each core (one last build-puzzle), the Hum swells back to full song, and the **Architect** rises to see whether you truly *understand* — a short, warm trial of everything you have learned. Pass it, and it shows you the empty chair and names you the next builder. The worlds light up across the horizon. The open world unlocks. **Your turn.** *(And the Spark in Pip's jar finally does the thing it was always for.)*

## 8. What we can build next

Concrete buildable pieces, roughly in priority order:

1. **The six Totem props** — small rigged voxel relics (Greenheart, Tideshell, Gilllight, Threetail, Wayfinder, Emberkin), each with an idle glow/pulse.
2. **The Village hub scene** — the Waystation: the Hearth + Lantern, six districts that light as totems are planted, the Ringwall. The cozy home screen between worlds.
3. **Gravemaw** — the missing Dustfall Warden (sand leviathan), to complete the six guardians.
4. **The two missing allies** — Myco (Mycelia) and Sift (Dustfall), so every world has a guide.
5. **The Architect** — the finale construct (calm, geometric, the warm empty chair).
6. **Hush-shard / anti-totem props** — the cleanse target and the visual language of the sleepy quiet.
7. **The wake-up moment** — the small scene of a Sleeper remembering its name and walking into the Village. The emotional core; worth getting just right.

### The 12-character cast (already prototyped)

A companion file, `pjboy-cast.js`, contains 12 fully rigged, animated Three.js (r128) NPCs for this cast — the six Kindled allies (Lumen, Pip, Bramblewick, Floe, Quill, Tinder) and the six Hushed (Hushling, Husk/Sleeper, Mute, Gnash, Riftling, Sentinel). It is **content-ready but not yet wired into the game**: it uses a `createCastKit(THREE, scene)` factory with states `idle | greet | alert`, whereas the live registries (`asteroid-creatures.js`, `asteroid-ships.js`) use the universal Actor contract `build() → {group, st, anim(t, state, dt)}` with states `idle | move | alert`. Integration work, when we get to it:

- Write a thin adapter so each cast NPC speaks the existing Actor contract, and route its FX through the shared `fx` kit in `asteroid-creatures.js` instead of its own duplicate helpers.
- **Dedup the `Sentinel`** — one already exists in `asteroid-creatures.js`; pick one owner.
- Reconcile the cast against the existing bestiary so a creature lives in exactly one registry.
- Bump the relevant `?v=N` in `index.html` and add the new script in the documented load order.

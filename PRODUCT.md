# Product

## Register

product

## Users

Casual players and families playing in the browser at home. Sessions are often shared-device, pick-up-and-play: parents and kids who want something fun without a steep learning curve. The primary job is to explore mazes, survive light combat, and optionally tinker in Create mode without reading a manual first.

## Product Purpose

PJBoy is a browser-based 3D second-person maze and action game with campaign, arena, open-world, and creator modes. It exists to deliver warm retro gaming in a single tab: approachable exploration and combat with enough depth for repeat visits. Success looks like players leaving a session feeling cozy and nostalgic, not stressed or lost in UI chrome.

## Brand Personality

Technical, professional, nerdy — expressed as warm retro craft, not corporate sterility or chaotic arcade noise. The interface should feel built by someone who loves game systems and wants to share that craft with newcomers. Tone is confident and clear: show how things work through layout and labels, not jargon or flashy decoration.

## Anti-references

- Modern SaaS dashboards (Linear, Notion, Stripe-style admin shells)
- Flat mobile-minimal UI (large white cards, soft gray neutrals, generic rounded buttons)
- Generic AI-slop patterns (gradient text, decorative glass cards, purple-on-white accent clichés)
- Interfaces that sacrifice readability for "modern" polish

## Design Principles

1. **Clarity over chrome** — HUD and menus stay readable at a glance; nothing competes with the 3D world for attention during play.
2. **Retro with intention** — Nostalgia serves usability. Effects and styling earn their place; they do not obscure state or controls.
3. **Family-first friction** — Every screen should be understandable without external docs. Mode names, buttons, and feedback favor plain language.
4. **Show the craft** — Nerdy personality lives in deliberate details (creator tools, mode structure, score feedback), not dense technical copy.
5. **Respect the player** — Accessibility and contrast are part of the product promise, not an afterthought bolted onto a retro skin.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

Target WCAG 2.1 AA where practical across menus and HUD: sufficient contrast on text and interactive controls, visible focus states, meaningful labels for icon-only or color-coded elements. Prefer patterns that do not rely on color alone for critical game state. When adding motion (glitch, flash, vignette), provide reduced-motion-friendly alternatives where feasible without breaking the retro identity.

## Reference Lane

Blend of PS1/N64-era game menus (chunky type, simple panels, warm not flashy) with early-2000s PC shareware tool clarity (utilitarian, nerdy, readable windows for settings and creator flows).
